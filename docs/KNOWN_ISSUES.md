# Known Issues and Limitations — v0.80.0

- **The headquarters (v0.88.0)** — the three offices can be bought, entered, kept open and worked in: officers have bodies, desks, lunch and cots, and the office has only as many seats (3, 8, 12) as it says. Not yet: furniture can be restyled but not moved (desks, floor, walls, lamps, pictures and plants only; the pictures are three simple palettes), the tower has one floor, an office cannot be sold, and officers do nothing but keep their routine (no tasks, no meetings with you). A game with no office has no seat limit. Desks (4, 12, 24) are fewer than the mockup's samples because the halls had to fit the free paving.
- **The OberPad (v0.85.0)** — finished: the home screen, the frame and every app follow the pattern (31–190 words a screen; the longest are Stocks at 189 words and 15 controls, a trading screen for three companies, and Group at 169). Behind a fold the old pages are as long as ever. The Group and Personnel summaries show only the most common actions (pay in, take out, bring a business in, see candidates); everything else is in the fold. **The tablet has not been tried on a touch device**: the touch and small-screen rules are written, not tested.
- **The early-game income is a first pass (v0.82.0)** — the shifts and first quests are tuned so a first arms works is within reach by day 3 (shown by arithmetic over the real figures, not by a scripted playthrough). The second-company-by-day-15 target is not measured, and no business's daily profit has been tuned end to end: dairy, the oil dividend, property rent, aerospace and the casino are unchanged, and the businesses can still run at a loss when nobody works them (a 25-day parity run with no one playing shows the arms works, the shipping line, the aerospace firm and the farm each losing money). Nothing like the starter loan, a cheaper first works or more early jobs is in yet. The figures are all in `src/content/income.js`.
- **A robbery lowers your standing with the Civic Office (−15)** — like burglary (−6) and theft (−8), it is faction standing: it reaches only the Civic Office's own dialogue (Osric's permit) and the prices of shops that faction owns (there are none). It is not read by any bank, loan, rating, valuation or ranking (source scans and the control game show it). The robbery also draws on the shared random sequence, so later dice in the wider world differ from a game without it.
- **After a reload the world's dice are re-rolled (found by `corporate_integration`; the same happens at v0.59.0, before this update, and the exact cause is not pinned down — the townspeople being re-settled on load is the likely one)** — a save keeps the shared random sequence, but the townspeople are re-settled when a game loads, so the arms market's wars, its orders, and so the world's competitors and the exchange's prices do not replay identically after a reload. The group's own records (accounts, loans, personnel, budgets, decisions) do. This does not duplicate or lose anything; it means a reload can change what the *world* does next, as it always could. Fixing it would mean making the townspeople's decisions independent of the shared sequence.
- **Not built (see `docs/CORPORATE_ACCEPTANCE.md`)**: selling or closing a subsidiary and part-owned subsidiaries; the central bank's balance sheet; emergency spending by managers; manager performance and budget-variance reports; department and facility assignments; reviews that weigh external conditions; recession milestones; the three older shares' own Stocks screen does not write to the trade record.

- **Bank robbery (v0.79.0)** — the central bank's "modeled financial loss" is its vault stock (notes and gold) and nothing more: there is **no central-bank balance sheet** (stage 10's "institutional balance sheet" was not built), so the loss is not booked against anything and the restocking is not paid for by anyone — it simply refills (this is where the brief's "no double counting" is easiest to keep). The vault stock exists only for the banks' own robbery records; no money is created or destroyed anywhere else. The bank closes only to the public: its tablet apps still work (they are the market), staff still turn up for work behind the tape, and the bond desk still opens the tablet. A sealed bank can be broken into again but its vault is sealed. The market reaction shows as a slightly lower screen value for bonds you already hold for ten days (lenders do not count it; see CORPORATE.md). There is no hostage, getaway-car or dye-pack mechanic; the guard fights and the staff run, nothing more. The *criminal reputation* is the police record and the civic reputation that already existed — no new notoriety number was built, because it was not asked for. The robbery interactions have only a browser check.

- **Staffed banks (v0.78.0)** — the clerks do not run the desks: the loan desk, the bond desk and the rate board still open the tablet by themselves, with or without the person behind them (a staffed counter that refuses service when its clerk is away, as the shops do, is not built). Staff cannot be hired, promoted or replaced, have no routine at all on the day a rate is decided, and do not react to a robbery yet. Crown Court's four residents take three flats, two of them flatmates. The stairwell and flats are plain (the same flat as every other home). The banks have no night guard: the doors are simply locked.

- **Foreign government bonds (v0.77.0)** — a country's *treasury* is the arms market's own budget (which only the arms trade moves), so a country's finances change slowly and a restructuring needs a heavy mix of causes (a broken treasury, two wars and an embargo, all at once, for forty days); in ordinary play it will be rare, which is the intent. The bonds pay their coupons from nothing, as every bond here does (the country is not a ledger entry anywhere), and a restructuring pays you a share of the face that no one loses. Holding a bond of a country under an arms embargo is allowed (the embargo is on arms). The debt trait is a fixed property of each country, not a number you can read in a source; the app says light/moderate/heavy. Delayed coupons are owed but earn nothing. There is no market in the bonds beyond buying from and selling to the issuer's price, and no price impact from your trades. The foreign-bond section has only a browser check.

- **What the wars cost (v0.76.0)** — the cost index touches only arms parts and aerospace materials and freight; there are no new costs for farming or shipping. The bond premium is a flat uncertainty and defence-borrowing premium on Halvermouth's bonds only; it does not model flight to safety, a neutral issuer's gain, or default (Halvermouth cannot default here). Managers respond only by asking for a larger *materials* budget (arms and aerospace, from a manager who reorders) and by holding back *expansion*; they do not change routes, suppliers or sales plans, and shipping and farm managers have no materials budget to ask about. A bid already placed before an embargo begins is not recalled. The thresholds (1% to ask, 2% to hold back, 70% of the budget used) are fixed numbers in `src/content/management.js`.

- **Wars and embargoes (v0.75.0)** — the events are the arms market's own wars and embargoes (it still decides, by its dice, when they begin and end); the layer records and prices them, and invents none of its own, so there are no commodity shocks, blockades or sanctions beyond those two kinds yet. The price response is a simplified model: demand and cost indices by sector against its margin, times a company's hashed exposure, bounded at ±12% of fair price; it does not know a company's real customers, so a generated company from a warring country is hit by being from it, not by a modelled loss of a named market. Arms demand already followed the war count before this release and is not counted again; an embargo only removes the target's income share from the arms pool. Your own arms works and aerospace firm pay the index on what they buy since v0.76.0; your farm and shipping line do not (their costs are the farm's and the voyage's own, and shipping already prices war into risk and insurance). The Economy screen has only a browser check.

- **Loans to companies (v0.74.0)** — only a company with its own funded account can borrow; a company's limit is a formula on its books (40% of what it is worth after all its debts, the bank's included, plus six months of its profit estimate), so a new company with little behind it can borrow nothing. The stated purpose of a loan is a label, not a restriction. A guarantee by you costs your record only (the bank never asks you to pay); a parent's guarantee is called once per missed instalment, only for what the parent holds. A company in arrears is cleared only when it repays (nothing is swept from its account); until then its managers do not hire, bid or expand (v0.74.0 to v0.77.0 swept its cash daily, which was a mistake and is gone). There is no collateral, refinancing, or loan for an acquisition as such. Net assets and debt count the bank's claim; the profit estimate still comes from the cash books, where interest is a cost. The company-loan screen has only a browser check.

- **Lariat State Bank loans (v0.73.0)** — personal loans only: the money comes from the bank and the interest goes back to it (no company's books and no account of anyone's are involved), so a loan does add cash to your wallet and a liability to the loans list; nothing shows debt in any net-worth figure because the game has none. The lender's building (`b_bank` in Lariat) is still a closed façade; the loans are made from the tablet and there are no bank staff. Collateral is valued at the price the app shows, before any selling fee or spread. A sale is refused by the lock only through the player's own sale routes: a takeover, a failure or a maturity can still leave you owing more than your holdings secure, and then nothing happens but that you cannot sell or borrow. Instalments are taken on the bank's day-start, not at a time you choose; there is no automatic top-up and no way to refinance. The Loans screen has only a browser check. Loans to companies and the later stages are not done.

- **The tablet's groups (v0.72.0)** — the badges are the notifications that have arrived since you last viewed Home, counted by the app they belong to; opening an app does not clear its badge, only viewing Home does (as before). Notifications from the arms trade's shipments and orders are counted under Business; world news under Economy. The search finds apps, not the things inside them (a company, a ticker, a person). The group and app bars have no automated test, only a browser check.

- **Corporate bonds (v0.71.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. **The bonds run beside the companies' books**: the cash an issue raises is not added to the company's cash, nor its coupons taken from its profit, so a company is worth and priced the same with or without bonds outstanding; the rating uses its real debt. A company only defaults when the world's simulation closes it, and then the recovery is the world's own rule, not negotiated. The rating is a model of the books (leverage, losses, cash, distress): it can lag, and a company can be AA and fail in a bad month. The bonds' interest and the face are paid to you out of nothing, as with government bonds and dividends (a larger spread for a riskier company is the only brake, with the one-twentieth cap). Bond values are not in the net worth quests read, nor the exchange portfolio. The corporate section has no automated test of its screens, only a browser check.

- **The physical bank (v0.70.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. There are **no tellers, governor or guard**: the hall is empty and the counter's windows say so; the vault door is a fixture that never opens; the hall is small (9 × 5 floor) because it is the size of the building on the street as it was drawn, and a bigger interior would move the street (and the hand-placed cars); the policy office is only the rate board. Open 09:00–17:00 every day (no weekends or holidays). The hall's furniture has no automated test beyond what is listed; its look was checked by eye in the browser.

- **Government bonds (v0.69.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. One issuer, no default, a fixed premium curve; a coupon is fixed when you buy and is not recalculated when the bank changes its rate (the value is). A bond bought and sold the same day costs the half-percent spread. The interest and the face are paid out of nothing (they are the government's): like dividends on shares, this puts money into your wallet that no company in the game paid, so a large holding earns without risk; the cap (2,000,000 face of each term) and the policy rate (usually near 5%) keep that modest, and a bank that cuts the rate takes the income down. Bond values are not counted in the net worth that quests and achievements read, and are not in the exchange's portfolio. The Bonds screens have no automated test, only a browser check.

- **The policy rate (v0.68.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. The rate follows only the wider world's companies (their busyness, sales and distress); it does not read your own businesses, and the game has no inflation measure for it to read. From day 91 on, valuations and so share prices move with it a little (a point above neutral is 3% off every multiple); a game that is not at 5% will differ from earlier releases in valuations, rankings and the exchange. Loans between your companies already made keep the rate they were made at. The bank's first meeting is on day 91 and a save made before that has no record. The Bank screens have no automated test, only a browser check.

- **The exchange (v0.66.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. Every listed company trades under one rule set (a 1% fee each way, a cap, a price that steps toward what its books say). Differences that remain on purpose or by necessity: **Chuckie's Cheese's cap is 1%** of the company, not 5% (Chuckie keeps control) and its daily noise uses the game's random numbers (as it always did); **Meridian** pays its dividend on Moondays and the others monthly or nightly as before; OBRO and CHZ's "worth on their books" is a model of the refinery and the dairy, not a valuation of accounts. **Ober's Oil moved from an instant price to a smoothed one in v0.67.0**, so a strike or an accident now takes the price down over days; shares you held before v0.67.0 and shares from projects have no cost on record, and the app shows no gain for them. (0.66.0 said CHZ had no fee or cap: wrong, see the changelog.) A wider-world share's price is the exchange's own opinion of the company's estimated value (net assets plus a multiple of a year's profit): it is a model, not a market; a company with no profit history is priced mostly on its assets. The exchange's share counts are its own and do not match the company records' counts. A holding's price history is sixty days; the board's history for OBRO is fourteen days, CHZ's and MRDN's as they keep it. A company that merges into another does not give you the buyer's shares — you are bought out in cash. A listing is made when its books can value it, so a company can be on the board a few days after it appears. The Exchange screens have no automated test, only a browser check.

- **Treasury (v0.65.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. One floor-and-ceiling policy per subsidiary, run by a chief financial officer at the parent; moves are only between the parent and one subsidiary. **A top-up is capital, not a loan**: it raises what the parent has put in, and a sweep can return it later. A top-up needs the parent to have cash above its reserve; with none it does nothing and the status says so only as "nothing to move" (the parent's balance is on the same screen). The ceiling and the monthly distribution policy are independent: a subsidiary can be swept by treasury and also send a distribution in the same month. Not yet: cash forecasts, interest on idle cash, debt and covenant monitoring (you did not choose them for this release). The Treasury controls have no automated test, only a browser check.

- **Bids and plans (v0.64.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. Managers bid on **aerospace tenders only** (shipping contracts and arms purchase orders are still yours). A bid is made at exactly the margin you set: how likely it is to win depends on that and on the customer's priorities, so a high margin wins less. A bid is sealed and final, so a manager's bid cannot be withdrawn. A plan's **placement is a fixed rule** and may not be where you would put it: an arms station goes in the first free cell with a clear ring around it, which can sit in the way of belts you plan later; **no worker is assigned** and **no recipe is set unless the plan names one**, so a new station idle until you staff it (it does not use parts while it has no worker). A farm plot is only ever *next to* one you own (within a lane, eight tiles), and never the first. Managers do not build halls, buy ships, fence or sow plots, or sell or close anything. A plan's price limit and reserve are checked when the item is bought; a price that rises between the quote and delivery is not re-checked for an arms order (it is paid at the order). Approving a queued bid or purchase re-plans it from today's figures: the price or the tender may have changed. The Management screens' new controls have no automated test, only a browser check.

- **Managers on their own (v0.63.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. Not yet: a reorder policy per business (the par is fixed: 8 hours of the staffed stations, reorder below half, 60 of a part at most); decisions by parent executives; shipping and farming reorders (their inputs are bought with your personal money by design); firing, raises or transfers by managers. **People hired in v0.62 keep the old limits** (a manager asks about nothing up to 10,000) until you lower them. The par for arms counts stations with a worker *assigned*, whether or not they are at the bench; a works whose belts never carry the parts off the dock simply stops reordering once the dock is above half of par. A hire's cost against the budget is the fee and a month's wage; later wages are payroll, not budget. Managers act only for businesses with their own account, so an unfunded business with a manager and a budget does nothing (the app says so). A decision refused for the same reason is written to the log once, so a standing refusal does not repeat in the log (the alert does). The Management screens have no automated test, only a browser check.

- **People (v0.62.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. **Authority** is what managers' own decisions are held to since v0.63.0 (nothing else reads it). **A manager's passive effect is small next to the wage in a small business** (a starting shipping fleet's upkeep is about 275 a day, and the Shipping and Logistics Manager's own ceiling is 10% of it — at most about 27 a day — against a wage of 130 or more; the 12% in the table is only the overall ceiling for upkeep, which a second person would have to supply): the app says what a hire is worth on today's figures and does not hide it. Effects are bounded by their ceilings and never apply without the thing they act on (no worker at a station, no plot, no firm). Arms floor staff are shown as they are and still managed at the works; aerospace crews are a head-count line. The displayed daily upkeep (the shipping terminal, the group's join preview) and the "worth" figure are worked out before a manager's discount; the discount shows only in what is actually paid. A parent executive's reach to a business joining or leaving the group is picked up the next day (the cached effect is keyed by day and by changes in people, not by group membership). The manufacturing manager's speed applies to every staffed arms station, including the research lab, so research is faster too. Skill growth comes only from good months (an employer beating the average of its previous three months, with the person paid) — in a business with no history there are none. Hiring is on the tablet only. Someone hired for a business you later put under the parent stays with it. Not yet: training, poaching, agencies, part-time or fixed-term contracts, anything a manager decides.

- **The group (v0.61.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. Not yet: selling, closing or restructuring a subsidiary beyond leaving the group; part-owned subsidiaries (every stake is 100%); a lender outside the group; the parent has no payroll, management expenses, debt or interest to pay (those lines read 0 until stages 6–8 and 12); no managers or budgets. **Elimination covers the one real intercompany flow** — shipping's freight charge to aerospace; satellites aerospace keeps as its own assets carry their share of that charge as an asset, and the charge is counted as realised once the unit that holds it is finished, so group profit from it is recognised a little early for those. Statements for arms, shipping and farming are on a cash basis and aerospace's from its books, as in v0.60. Intercompany interest and dividends post in the month they are paid (the first morning after the month they are for). After a gap of more than three months only the last three run their loan and distribution jobs. The loan rate is the benchmark plus a point, a fixed number until a central bank exists. Joining converts a business to its own account *with whatever opening capital you give it*: with none, an arms business's staff go unpaid and quit, aerospace and shipping fall into arrears, a farm's purchases are refused — the app warns first, and these are real consequences. The group's value is exact only while its companies are not close to worthless (a value has a floor). Items of your §20.5 integrated test that need managers (1–3, 8, 9, 11) wait for stage 7; items 4–7, 10 and 12 are tested now.

- **Personal money (v0.60.1)** — by design these stay on your wallet whatever you fund: shares and dividends, everything bought in a shop (farm seed, feed and fertiliser come from your pockets), cars and tractors from the dealers, homes and plots, the calculator factory, fines, bribes and protection money, a day's labour and the casino. A funded farm therefore pays for land, fencing and animals but not for the supplies you carry in. Buying a business itself is always your equity.

- **Corporate empire, stages 2–4 (v0.60.0)** — see docs/CORPORATE.md. The wider world's companies are an *abstract* model, not people and plants: they sell a share of a sector's daily demand and keep books, but do not bid on individual tenders, buy named materials or change their prices (their price level is fixed at birth); wars and embargoes touch them only through the arms pool (the sim already takes a per-sector cost index; stage 13 will fill it). They keep no backlog, inventory or staff figures, so those columns don't exist yet. Ober's Oil and the freight carriers keep no revenue or profit at all: they are listed with "—". Chuckie's Cheese is counted at today's run-rate. Arms, shipping and farming figures are on a **cash basis** (what moved through the company's account, shipping's charges from the aerospace division left out); they are not accrual accounts, so a month with a big purchase shows it as spending, not as an asset. Estimated values count the owner's plant at 60% of list price (land at cost) and trust a year's profit only as far as six months of history; a legacy save's businesses show no history until the months pass. A funded company's bills that it cannot pay become arrears with no interest and no lender — loans, a central bank and a policy rate are later stages. The home country has no name in the lore; it is called Halvermouth. Renaming shipping does not change the text of the logistics terminal's old greeting (it reads the company that was bought). Not yet: parent companies and subsidiaries, managers and budgets, the generated companies' share prices, the central bank, bonds, loans, wars beyond arms, the bank robbery.

- **Aerospace, stage V — services, research, reports (v0.59.0)** — a satellite of your own needs your own pad, a rocket you can build and a clean room (the same as a customer's); Meridian doesn't run networks of its own, only the support fees for its customers' satellites. Markets are one world-wide pool of demand with unnamed rivals — no geography, no individual customers, no negotiating. Your own orders are standard configurations (the hardened bus and the long-life options come on customers' tenders). Insurance is one flat policy for the whole fleet. The Orbit picture is a schematic: orbital radii are squeezed, the continents are made up, and the satellites you have not got are shown only for company. The debris decision is the only one the sky asks of you. Reports: cash flow and monthly figures start with this version (an older save has none for what came before), Roost's cash isn't on its balance sheet (it spends from your own account) and its plant is counted at 60% of cost without depreciation. Achievements on an older save are awarded on the next morning for what the record already shows (and pay their grants then). Achievement grants come from nowhere in particular and are not in the books.

## Limitations of current systems

- NPCs do not physically collide with the player or each other (they pass through; it avoids deadlocks in narrow interiors) — so in a fight, attackers can stand inside you.
- Killing a story character can make quests impossible (by design, they fail). Dialogue elsewhere may still mention a dead character as if alive (lines are not yet death-aware).
- Fleeing NPCs run in a straight line away from you and can get stuck in corners until their fear wears off (~45 game minutes).
- The Debug menu is available to everyone who plays (it's a testing tool, not hidden); saves made with it are flagged `debug_used`. `time` only moves the clock forward. Teleporting into a locked interior bypasses its lock.
- The Wrath cutscene can't be skipped (about 10 seconds). Dialogue doesn't yet react to `smitten_by_<id>` (the flag is recorded for future lines).
- The Wrath's lightning starts above the top of the screen; in tall interiors it appears through the ceiling (intentionally: it's the Oberverse).
- Gunshots only alarm people on the same map; a fight in a building is not heard outside.
- Facts are permanent once known; repeated events reuse the same fact id (e.g. a second bang does not "refresh" news for someone who already heard of the first). Event chains that depend on fresh news may therefore trigger from old knowledge.
- Abstract-tier travel uses Manhattan-distance estimates, so off-screen journeys can be slightly faster or slower than walking them.
- The game clock pauses during dialogue and menus; NPCs freeze while you talk to anyone.
- The notice board is the only "public" information channel; there are no letters/telephones yet.
- Reputation changes from facts about the player apply once per fact, the first time anyone else learns it.
- Music is synthesised; quality is intentional but not production-grade (see `docs/AUDIO_GUIDE.md`).
- Text is English only; no localisation layer.

- Sir Moneybucks's walk from the manor to the Quay takes ~45 game minutes; on the abstract tier the estimate can vary by ±10 minutes.
- During a strike, picketing workers stand at the gate but the refinery interior is simply empty (no "scab" behaviour yet).
- The Ledger can only be played once per day, and a match abandoned mid-way keeps the stake.
- Region seams are east–west only; maps can't yet be joined north–south.
- Skyscrapers along the northern edge of Halvermouth are cut off by the top of the map (the camera stops at the map edge).
- Halvermouth's static ground layer is large (3584×2560 px); fine on desktop Chromium (60 fps measured), not yet measured on phones or tablets.
- When NPCs are added to Halvermouth, they will need routes through the gateway doors (cross-region travel uses the door graph; untested with residents).
- Across the seam, the off-map neighbour's NPCs are drawn but you can't talk to them until you step across (interactions use the current map).
- Sleeping NPCs are drawn standing with closed eyes, not lying in bed.
- The Mason's Os site never changes (as requested); the crew's "build" impulse sends them to one spot on the Lots whatever plot is being built.
- The excavator is not solid (Mason sits in its cab); the player can walk through it.
- Mason's afternoon trip to the Civic Office is a long walk from the site office, so he spends part of his permit hour walking; off-screen the trip is estimated.

## Defects

Fixed in 0.2.0 (found while building it):
- Event templates had no `id`, so all events shared one cooldown slot; "once" events could be blocked by any other event. Saves from 0.1.x may contain a harmless `lastFired.undefined` entry.
- Several personality impulses could never outscore schedules (e.g. Mags's patio natter); weights corrected and the validator now rejects impossible impulses.

None known otherwise. Report new ones here with steps to reproduce.
